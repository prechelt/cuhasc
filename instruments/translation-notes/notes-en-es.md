# CVscale translation notes: English → Spanish

**Source language:** English · **Target language:** Spanish (español)

Files written to `instruments/`:
- `cvscale-es.tsv` — 26 items across the five groups
- `dimensions-es.csv` — dimension names, English kept after " / "
- `scales-es.csv` — conventional Likert anchors (`totalmente en desacuerdo`…`totalmente de acuerdo`; `muy poco importante`…`muy importante`)

The translation was mostly straightforward — Spanish's Latinate roots let each item map closely, and the impersonal third-person framing sidestepped the tú/usted register problem. Three decisions were worth flagging:

## 1. CO — "Individuals" (SEM vs. gender-neutrality)

Spanish forces grammatical gender and has no truly neutral plural noun. Variants: **A) "los individuos"** (generic masculine, unmarked) vs. **B) "las personas"** ("people," grammatically feminine but neutral in reference). B reads as more gender-neutral, but the CO dimension is *Collectivism vs. Individualism* — "individuo" sits squarely in that semantic field while "personas" dilutes it. I chose **A**: SEM was decisive, and generic masculine keeps gender effectively invisible in the generic reading (Spanish convention). Shared stem "individu-" also lets me honor UNI across the noun ("los individuos") and adjective ("recompensas/objetivos individuales").

## 2. UN2 — "closely follow" (AMB)

Variants: **A) "seguir de cerca"** (follow closely) vs. **B) "seguir al pie de la letra"** (follow to the letter) / "seguir estrictamente" (strictly). B is stronger than the source ("strongly/strictly" ≠ "closely"), violating CON. A carries a mild ambiguity — "seguir de cerca" can also mean *monitor closely* — but English "closely follow" has the same latent range, so under LAM/AMB the ambiguity is present in the source too and isn't newly introduced. Chose **A** as the closest match in strength.

## 3. LT1 — "Thrift" gloss (CON)

Variants for the parenthetical: **A) "Ahorro"** (saving) vs. **B) "Frugalidad"** (frugality). "Thrift" is a positively-connoted virtue; B risks an austerity/self-denial overtone the neutral English lacks, while A is the conventional, positively-valenced virtue term (matching German "Sparsamkeit"). Chose **A** on CON.

## Structural note

I rendered "success" as **éxito** everywhere it recurs (CO4 and LT5/LT6), and "welfare of the group" identically in CO3/CO5 (`el bienestar del grupo`), to preserve the source's within-group uniformity (UNI).
