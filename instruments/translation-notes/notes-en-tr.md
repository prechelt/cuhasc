# CVscale translation notes: English → Turkish

**Source language:** English · **Target language:** Turkish (Türkçe)

Files written to `instruments/`:
- `cvscale-tr.tsv` — 26 items across the five groups
- `dimensions-tr.csv` — dimension names, English kept after " / "
- `scales-tr.csv` — conventional Likert anchors (`kesinlikle katılmıyorum`…`kesinlikle
  katılıyorum`; `çok önemsiz`…`çok önemli`)

## Target-language characterization

- **Register / variety.** Turkish has a single, strongly standardized written norm (Istanbul
  Turkish) with little regional split in formal writing, so variety-stability was not a
  concern. A neutral, mildly formal questionnaire register was used throughout.
- **Gender & honorifics.** This is Turkish's decisive *advantage* here: the language has no
  grammatical gender and a single gender-neutral third-person pronoun (`o`). The first-person
  self-report items (UN1, UN3) that force gender agreement in Russian/Arabic expose nothing in
  Turkish — `bilirim` ("I know") is neutral. The only gendered wording is the MA group, where
  the source itself names men and women (`erkek`/`kadın`).
- **Derivational morphology.** Turkish is agglutinative with transparent roots, which makes
  UNI easy to honor: `birey` ("individual") underlies both `bireyler` (CO "individuals") and
  `bireysel` (CO "individual rewards/success"), mirroring the source's own repetition and
  sitting squarely on the individualism pole (SEM).
- **Polysemy / register layering.** The main watch-point was words carrying a second sense
  English lacks — notably `zorlayıcı` ("forcible" *and* "challenging/difficult"), avoided in
  MA3 (see below).
- **Connotation & culture.** No systematic amplification in ordinary Turkish for the PO/CO/MA
  loadings; individual word choices still needed CON checks (LT1 "thrift", PO4 "disagree",
  CO6 "loyalty").

## 1. MA3 — "active, forcible approach" (AMB / CON)

"Forcible" wanted the natural candidate **B)** `zorlayıcı`, but `zorlayıcı` is ambiguous in
Turkish: besides "forcing/compelling" it very commonly means "challenging / hard-to-do" — an
ambiguity the English "forcible" does not have (AMB), and one that would fatally blur an item
about a *forceful manner*. **A)** `güçlü` ("strong, forceful") keeps the forceful-manner sense
without the difficulty reading. The literal `güç kullanan` ("force-using") was rejected as
over-physical (CON). I chose **A** (`aktif, güçlü bir yaklaşım`) on AMB.

## 2. PO4 — "should not disagree with decisions" (CON + naturalness)

The scale root `katıl-` ("agree") cannot render *negated* "disagree" cleanly here —
`katılmamalıdır` means "should not *agree*", the opposite of what is wanted, and a faithful
"should not dis-agree" produces an awkward double negative. Candidates for "disagree with":
**A)** `karşı çıkmak` ("to go against / object to") vs. **B)** `karşı gelmek` ("to defy"). **B**
overshoots into open defiance (CON). I chose **A** (`kararlarına karşı çıkmamalıdır`); it is
the natural rendering of disagreeing-with-a-decision and stays at the source's moderate strength.

## 3. LT3 — "Personal steadiness and stability" (near-synonym pair)

English pairs two overlapping notions, but Turkish `istikrar` covers *both* "steadiness" and
"stability", so a one-to-one pair is hard. Candidates for "steadiness": `tutarlılık`
("consistency", leans logical), `kararlılık` ("resolve" — already used for LT2 `kararlılıkla`,
so reusing it would forge a false UNI link the source doesn't make), and **`sağlamlık`**
("soundness / being solidly steady"). I chose `Kişisel sağlamlık ve istikrar`, pairing
`sağlamlık` (steadiness) with `istikrar` (stability) and avoiding the LT2 collision.

## Minor connotation / lexical checks

- **CO6 "loyalty" → `grup bağlılığı`** (commitment/attachment to the group) over `sadakat`
  ("fidelity/allegiance"), which leans toward sworn faithfulness; `bağlılık` carries the warm
  belonging sense of collectivist "group loyalty" (CON). CO2 "stick with the group" was kept
  as `grupla birlikte kalmak` ("stay together with"), *not* a loyalty word, to preserve the
  source's separation between CO2 and CO6.
- **LT1 "Thrift" → `Tutumluluk`**, the positively-valenced virtue, over `cimrilik`
  ("stinginess/miserliness") (CON).
- **LT2 "Persistence" gloss → `Azim`** (perseverance) rather than `Kararlılık`, since
  "resolutely" was already rendered `kararlılıkla`; distinct roots keep gloss and adverb apart.
- **PO "positions" → `daha yüksek/düşük konumdaki kişiler`**, a neutral hierarchical phrase
  applied uniformly across all five PO items (UNI); preferred over `üstler/astlar`
  ("superiors/subordinates"), which narrow to formal rank.
- **UN "instructions" → `talimat`** uniformly across UN1, UN2, UN5 (UNI); `prosedür` for
  "procedures" across UN2, UN4.
- **CO "success" → `başarı`** repeated in CO4 and LT5/LT6, and **"solve problems" → `problemleri
  çözer`** repeated in MA2, matching the source's own parallelism (UNI).

## Overall

Turkish matches the instrument very well; the translation was largely straightforward. Its
lack of grammatical gender removes the single biggest structural difficulty other languages
face here (the first-person items and the agreement scale stay effortlessly neutral). The only
genuine judgment calls were lexical: the `zorlayıcı`/`güçlü` ambiguity in MA3, the negated
"disagree" in PO4, and the steadiness/stability near-synonym pair in LT3.
