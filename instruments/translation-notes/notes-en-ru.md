# CVscale translation notes: English → Russian

**Source language:** English · **Target language:** Russian (русский)

Files written to `instruments/`:
- `cvscale-ru.tsv` — 26 items across the five groups
- `dimensions-ru.csv` — dimension names, English kept after " / "
- `scales-ru.csv` — conventional Likert anchors (`полностью не согласен`…`полностью согласен`; `совсем неважно`…`очень важно`)

## Target-language characterization

- **Register / variety.** Russian has a single, highly standardized written norm with little
  regional split, so variety-stability was not a concern. Register mattered instead: the
  neutral, slightly formal questionnaire register was used throughout.
- **Derivational morphology.** Rich, transparent stems make UNI easy to honor *and* easy to
  over-honor. The `индивид-`/`индивидуальн-` family let me link CO "individuals" to
  "individual rewards/success" (as the source does), and `береж-` links `бережное` to
  `бережливость` in LT1. I watched that shared stems did not forge links the English keeps
  apart.
- **Polysemy / register layering.** Few false friends here; the main watch-point was
  loanword vs. native pairs (`лояльность` vs. `верность`, `делегировать` vs. `поручать`),
  where the native term is warmer and the loanword more clinical (see CON notes).
- **Connotation & culture.** No systematic amplification risk in ordinary Russian for the
  PO/CO/MA loadings, but individual word choices needed CON checks (LT1 "thrift",
  LT2 "resolutely", UN2 "closely", CO6 "loyalty").
- **Gender & honorifics.** This is the one structural pressure point. Russian forces gender
  agreement on predicate short adjectives and past-tense verbs. Wherever the respondent
  speaks in the first person the natural wording would expose gender; I restructured to
  keep it invisible (see UN1). The agreement scale itself cannot avoid it — see below.

## 1. Scale anchor `disagree5` — grammatical gender of "agree" (gender rule vs. convention)

The conventional Russian 5-point agreement anchor is `полностью не согласен` … `полностью
согласен`, but `согласен` is a **masculine** short adjective (feminine `согласна`). Variants:
**A)** the conventional masculine `согласен` (what scientific Russian questionnaires actually
print) vs. **B)** an inclusive `согласен(-на)`, which is fully gender-neutral but non-standard
and visually clumsy. The skill's scale rule ("use the conventional scientific translation")
and its gender-neutrality rule genuinely conflict here, because no short, neutral, *conventional*
form exists. I chose **A**, matching established Russian survey practice; the masculine is the
lexicographically unmarked default. (The `important5` anchor has no such problem — `важно`/
`неважно` are neuter predicatives and stay neutral.)

## 2. UN1 — "so that I always know what I'm expected to do" (hiding respondent gender)

A literal "so that I always know" is `чтобы я всегда знал` (m) / `знала` (f) — gender-exposed
in the past/subjunctive. Variants: **A)** impersonal `чтобы мне всегда было понятно, что от
меня ожидается` ("so that it is always clear to me what is expected of me") vs. **B)** the
gendered `чтобы я всегда знал(а)…`. I chose **A**: the impersonal `было понятно` +
passive `ожидается` removes every agreement slot, keeping gender invisible without changing
the meaning. The same passive `что от меня ожидается` is reused in UN3 (`сообщают мне, что
от меня ожидается`) for UNI, since both source items share "what is expected of me".

## 3. CO "Individuals" — SEM vs. naturalness (`индивиды` vs. `люди`/`человек`)

Variants: **A)** `индивиды` ("individuals," somewhat academic in everyday Russian) vs.
**B)** the more natural `люди` ("people") or generic singular `человек`. The CO dimension is
*Collectivism vs. Individualism*, so `индивид` sits inside the semantic field (SEM) and, via
its stem, lets the contrasts read as `индивидуальных вознаграждений` (CO3) and
`индивидуального успеха` (CO4) — mirroring the source's own "individual … individual"
repetition (UNI). `люди`/`человек` would read more smoothly but severs that link and dilutes
the individualism pole. I chose **A** on SEM+UNI; the register is defensible in a psychometric
instrument.

## Minor connotation / lexical checks

- **CO6 "loyalty" → `верность группе`** (native, "faithfulness to the group") over the
  loanword `лояльность`. `лояльность` in modern Russian leans toward neutral compliance/
  non-hostility, whereas "group loyalty" in the collectivism frame is the warmer allegiance
  sense that `верность` carries (CON). Avoided `преданность` ("devotion"), which overshoots.
- **LT1 "Thrift" → `бережливость`**, the positively-valenced virtue term, over `экономность`
  (thriftiness with a faint stinginess tinge) or `скупость` (miserliness) (CON). The stem
  `береж-` also echoes `бережное обращение` in the same item.
- **LT2 "resolutely / opposition" → `решительное … вопреки сопротивлению`**, matching the
  source's strength; avoided `упорное` (which duplicates the `настойчивость` gloss) and the
  stronger `яростное сопротивление`.
- **UN2 "closely follow" → `точно следовать`** rather than `строго`/`неукоснительно
  следовать` ("strictly / rigidly"), which overshoot the moderate "closely" (CON).
- **PO "positions" → `занимающие более высокое/низкое положение`**, a neutral hierarchical
  phrase applied uniformly to all five PO items (UNI). Preferred over `вышестоящие/
  нижестоящие`, which narrow the sense to formal rank/superior-subordinate.
- **PO4 "disagree" → `выражать несогласие`** (mild) rather than `оспаривать` ("challenge/
  dispute," stronger); a bare `не соглашаться` would produce the awkward double negative
  `не должны не соглашаться` (CON + naturalness).
- **MA2** repeats `решают проблемы` in both clauses exactly as the source repeats "solve
  problems" (UNI/parallelism).

## Overall

Russian matches most items closely; the translation was largely straightforward. The only
structural difficulty is grammatical gender (items 1 and 2 above), handled by impersonal
restructuring where the respondent speaks, and by accepting the conventional masculine in the
one place — the agreement scale — where no neutral conventional form exists.
