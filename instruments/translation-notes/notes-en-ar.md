# Translation notes: English → Arabic (Modern Standard Arabic)

**Source language:** English
**Target language:** Arabic — rendered in **Modern Standard Arabic (MSA / fusha)**.

## Why MSA

Arabic is strongly diglossic: every region has a spoken dialect (Egyptian, Levantine,
Gulf, Maghrebi, …) that diverges from the written standard in vocabulary and grammar, and
the dialects are not mutually stable. For a scientific questionnaire meant to be comparable
across the whole Arabic-speaking world, MSA is the only register that stays constant across
respondents, so the entire instrument is in MSA. This directly serves the "wording stable
across the varieties respondents span" requirement.

## Target-language characterization (axes that stressed the rules)

- **Register split:** handled by committing to MSA throughout (see above).
- **Derivational morphology:** Arabic's root-and-pattern system makes UNI easy to honor but
  also easy to violate by accident — words sharing a root read as linked. I used this
  deliberately for the recurring pairs (المناصب العليا / المناصب الدنيا for higher/lower
  positions; المجموعة for "the group"; التعليمات / الإجراءات for instructions/procedures;
  رفاه المجموعة for "group welfare/welfare of the group"; النجاح في المستقبل for "success in
  the future") and watched for unintended root echoes (see MA3/scale note below).
- **Polysemy / religious-classical layering:** the neutral administrative vocabulary chosen
  (منصب, تعليمات, إجراءات, لوائح, ولاء) carries no unwanted classical or religious secondary
  sense, so no AMB/LAM problems arose there.
- **Connotation & culture (CON):** watched authority valence in PO and gender valence in MA
  so as not to amplify the source (details below).
- **Gender:** MSA marks gender grammatically. All items except the MA group avoid naming a
  gendered subject (المناصب / الأفراد / المجموعة are gender-neutral), so no gender leaks in.
  The MA group contrasts men and women by design, so gender is inherent, not an artifact.

## Most difficult decisions

### 1. PO4 — "should not disagree with decisions" (CON, UNI)
Candidates for "disagree with": **A** يعترضوا على (object to), **B** يخالفوا (differ from),
**C** يعارضوا (oppose). "يعترض على" leans toward *object/protest* — stronger and more overt
than the source's mild "disagree." "يخالف" is close to *differ* but sits awkwardly with
"decisions." I chose **يعترضوا على القرارات**... on balance: it is the natural collocation with
"decisions" and reads as ordinary questionnaire language, and I judged the small extra
overtness acceptable versus the awkwardness of the alternatives. Note the Likert anchor uses
أعارض for "disagree," so I deliberately did *not* reuse عارض in the item to avoid forging a
spurious UNI link between an item and the response scale.

### 2. LT1 — "Careful management of money (Thrift)" (CON, gloss)
"Thrift" is frugality/economy, a mildly positive virtue, not mere "saving." Candidates for
the gloss: **A** التوفير (economizing/thrift), **B** الادّخار (saving/putting money aside),
**C** الاقتصاد (economy). الادّخار names the act of accumulating savings, which is narrower
than the dispositional virtue "thrift"; الاقتصاد is broad and can read as the discipline
"economics." I chose **التوفير** as the closest single-word match to the frugality sense. For
the head phrase "careful management" I used الإدارة الحصيفة (prudent management) — حصيف
carries exactly the "careful, judicious" valence without drifting into "stingy."

### 3. MA3 — "an active, forcible approach, which is typical of men" (CON)
"Forcible" is the delicate word: it means *forceful/using force*, and MSA offers حازم
(firm/decisive), قوي (strong/forceful), or عنيف (violent/aggressive). عنيف overshoots into
aggression; حازم undershoots toward mere decisiveness. I paired نشط (active) with **قوي**
(forceful) to match the source's strength without tipping into violence, and rendered "typical
of men" as يتّسم به الرجال عادةً (that men are usually characterized by) — a descriptive
"typical," avoiding any normative "should," so the gender claim keeps the source's valence and
is not amplified.

## Overall

Apart from the three items above, the translation was largely straightforward: the PO/UN/CO
groups map cleanly onto stable MSA administrative vocabulary and the root system let me hold
the intended within-group uniformity without strain. The LT phrases translate as
noun phrases matching the source's phrase form, and the MA group's inherent gender contrast
transfers directly. The Likert anchors use the conventional Arabic scientific-questionnaire
wording (أعارض بشدة / أوافق بشدة; غير مهمّ إطلاقاً / مهمّ جداً).
