# CVscale translation notes: English → French

**Source language:** English · **Target language:** French (français)

Files written to `instruments/`:
- `cvscale-fr.tsv` — 26 items across the five groups
- `dimensions-fr.csv` — dimension names, English kept after " / "
- `scales-fr.csv` — conventional Likert anchors (`pas du tout d'accord`…`tout à fait d'accord`; `très peu important`…`très important`)

## Target-language characterization

French is a Latinate language whose formal written standard is highly stable across
regions (France, Belgium, Switzerland, Québec, Africa) — the register that questionnaires
use varies little, so responses stay comparable. The impersonal third-person framing of
most items sidesteps the tu/vous problem; only UN1 and UN3 speak in the first person, and
there the neutral "je … ce que l'on attend de moi" is register-safe everywhere. Shared
Latin roots make UNI easy to honor (individu/individuel, groupe, réussite) but also easy
to violate by accident, so I watched for stems that would forge a link the source keeps
separate (see CO2 vs. CO6 below). French forces grammatical gender on nouns, which raises
the same "individuals" problem Spanish has.

The translation was largely straightforward. Three decisions were worth flagging.

## 1. CO2 "stick with the group" vs. CO6 "loyalty" (UNI / SEM)

The risk was translating CO2 with a loyalty word and thereby merging it with CO6, where
"loyalty" is the actual concept. Variants for "stick with the group even through
difficulties": **A) "rester solidaires du groupe"** (stay in solidarity with the group)
vs. **B) "rester fidèles au groupe"** (stay loyal/faithful to the group). B reuses the
*fidélité/loyauté* field that belongs to CO6 ("La loyauté envers le groupe…"), collapsing
two distinct items. A keeps CO2 about cohesion/solidarity — squarely inside the
Collectivism field (SEM) yet lexically distinct from CO6. Chose **A**: preserving the
source's separation of the two items (UNI, negatively applied) was decisive.

## 2. PO4 "disagree with decisions" (CON)

Variants: **A) "contester les décisions"** (challenge/dispute the decisions) vs.
**B) "être en désaccord avec les décisions"** (be in disagreement with the decisions).
B is the literal, mildest match but is syntactically heavy after "ne devraient pas". A is
idiomatic and natural but slightly stronger — "contester" implies voicing/acting on the
disagreement, not merely holding it. Since the whole PO item is precisely about whether
subordinates should *voice* dissent to superiors' decisions, that active nuance is on-
topic rather than an over-translation, and "contester" is the term French management usage
would reach for. Chose **A**, judging the strength gap acceptable under CON and outweighed
by naturalness.

## 3. MA4 "jobs … a man can do better" (SEM / collocation)

Variants for "jobs": **A) "métiers"** (occupations/trades) vs. **B) "emplois"** (job
positions) vs. **C) "tâches/travaux"** (tasks). C narrows to discrete tasks and drifts
from the professional-career framing that MA1 establishes for the group; B ("emplois")
foregrounds the employment slot rather than the kind of work. A ("métiers") keeps the
occupational reading that fits Masculinity's career/role field (SEM) and collocates
naturally with "faire mieux". Chose **A**: "Il y a certains métiers qu'un homme fera
toujours mieux qu'une femme."

## Structural / uniformity notes

- "success" is rendered **réussite** everywhere it recurs (CO4, LT5, LT6) for UNI; "success
  in the future" becomes "la réussite future" in both LT5 and LT6.
- "welfare of the group" / "group welfare" is identical in CO3 and CO5 ("le bien-être du
  groupe"); "goals/objectives" is "objectifs" in CO5 and CO6.
- "instructions" is kept across UN1, UN2, UN5 (UN5 "operations" → "instructions relatives
  aux opérations") to preserve the source's within-group repetition.
- LT glosses: "Thrift" → **Épargne** (the positively-valenced saving/virtue term, avoiding
  the austerity overtone of *frugalité*), matching the Spanish "Ahorro" / German
  "Sparsamkeit" choice; "Persistence" → **Persévérance**, the standard French Hofstede term.
- Dimension names use the conventional French management-literature terms: *distance
  hiérarchique* (Power Distance) and *contrôle de l'incertitude* (Uncertainty Avoidance).
- French typographic spacing before the semicolon is applied in MA2.
