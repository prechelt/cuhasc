# CVscale translation notes: English → Indonesian

**Source language:** English · **Target language:** Indonesian (Bahasa Indonesia)

Files written to `instruments/`:
- `cvscale-id.tsv` — 26 items across the five groups
- `dimensions-id.csv` — dimension names, English kept after " / "
- `scales-id.csv` — conventional Likert anchors (`sangat tidak setuju`…`sangat setuju`; `sangat tidak penting`…`sangat penting`)

## Target-language characterization

Indonesian suits this instrument well. It is **grammatically gender-neutral** (no gendered
nouns, neutral `dia`), so gender stays invisible everywhere except the MA group, where I mark it
explicitly with the standard neutral pair `laki-laki` / `perempuan`. Its **rich derivational
morphology** makes UNI easy to honor from shared roots — `hasil` → `keberhasilan` (success),
`atur` → `aturan`/`peraturan` (rules/regulations). The **standard written register** is stable
across regions, so responses stay comparable; I avoided colloquial forms. The main watch-points
were **polysemy**: `petunjuk` (instructions) can also mean "clue," and `operasi` can mean
"surgery/military operation" — both were steered around by context.

The translation was largely straightforward. Three decisions were worth flagging.

## 1. PO4 — "disagree with decisions" (SEM / CON)

Variants: **A) `membantah keputusan`** (contradict / object to / talk back against a decision)
vs. **B) `tidak setuju dengan keputusan`** (literally "not agree with the decision").
B is the most literal rendering of "disagree," but it echoes the response scale itself
(`setuju` = agree), and — more importantly — silent private disagreement is not what PO4 targets.
Within the Power Distance frame the item is about subordinates *not challenging* a superior's
decision, i.e. deference to authority. **A** sits squarely in that semantic field (SEM) and its
strength — object to / talk back, not full "oppose" (`menentang`, which would overshoot on CON) —
matches the source. I chose **A**, with SEM decisive.

## 2. UN — "instructions" as `petunjuk` (UNI vs. AMB)

"Instructions" recurs in UN1, UN2, and UN5. Candidates: **A) `petunjuk`** (guidance/instructions)
vs. **B) `instruksi`** (loanword, directive command). `petunjuk` is the natural, neutral term and
lets me keep UNI across all three items, but it carries a secondary sense "clue/hint" (an AMB
concern the English lacks). I judged that sense fully suppressed by each context
(`petunjuk yang dijabarkan secara rinci`, `petunjuk dan prosedur`, `petunjuk pengoperasian` all
read unambiguously as instructions), whereas `instruksi` tilts toward a military/command tone that
overloads the neutral English (CON). Chose **A**: UNI plus register fit outweigh a latent ambiguity
that never actually surfaces. Relatedly, UN5's "operations" became `pengoperasian` (operating) rather
than `operasi`, which would have imported an unwanted "surgery/military op" reading.

## 3. CO6 — "individual goals suffer" (CON)

Variants for "suffer": **A) `terhambat`** (be hindered/impeded) vs. **B) `dikorbankan`**
(be sacrificed) vs. **C) `dirugikan`** (be harmed). B collides with CO1, where "sacrifice" is
already `mengorbankan`, and implies deliberate giving-up rather than the source's passive setback.
C ("harmed") is an awkward collocation with `tujuan` (goals). **A** `terhambat` is the natural
intransitive collocation for a goal being set back and matches the mild, passive valence of
"suffer" (CON). Chose **A**.

## Structural note

"Success" is rendered `keberhasilan` everywhere it recurs (CO4 ×2, LT5, LT6); "group" is `kelompok`
and "welfare of the group" is `kesejahteraan kelompok` in both CO3 and CO5; "should" is `sebaiknya`
throughout the PO and CO prescriptive items — all to preserve the source's within-group uniformity
(UNI). The LT items are kept as phrases (no verb-of-obligation, no closing period), and their
parenthetical glosses are translated as positively-valenced virtue terms: `(Hemat)` for Thrift and
`(Kegigihan)` for Persistence (CON).
